<objective>
Give the purge the same two-process dead-man's switch every other destructive background job in this system has. The worker writes its progress to `purge_records`; an API-side watchdog reads that table on an interval and raises exactly one deduplicated operator alert when a purge has been running past a threshold without progressing, or has recorded a failure. Success raises nothing — the structured completion log line and the durable purge record are the evidence, and alerts stay reserved for failure so the channel keeps meaning something.

The mechanism is entirely borrowed: `partition-watchdog.ts`'s evaluate-then-claim-then-send shape, `ops_alert_state`'s multi-replica dedup claim, and `check:runbook-coverage`'s convention that every declared alert name has a runbook file on disk. The only new thing is the health predicate — and the false-positive cases matter as much as the true ones, because a purge legitimately sits in `reported` for a whole tick by design (D-07) and legitimately takes a long time on a large tenant.

Purpose: D-08's observability clause under PRG-01 (unattended purge must not fail silently) and PRG-03 (a stalled resumable job must be visible).
Output: the watchdog module, its registration, its runbook, and a test suite whose false-positive controls are as thorough as its alert cases.
</objective>
