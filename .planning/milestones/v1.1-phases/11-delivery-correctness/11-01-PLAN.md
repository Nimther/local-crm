## Assumption-delta decision

The `assumption-delta` detector fired (`pluralization` on "a second terminal write" / "another worker", `chosen` on `custom_args`). The identity-model question it surfaces, answered explicitly:

- **The noun that is now primary:** the **send intent** (workspace + campaign + contact, or workspace + flow run + node), not the **send attempt**. `sends.id` stops being "the id of this insert attempt" and becomes "the id of this intent" (D-09, implemented in 11-04).
- **Decision: `promote`.** The intent identity is promoted to the primary key; "attempt" is demoted to a non-identity detail that lives entirely in BullMQ's own `attemptsMade`, never on the `sends` row. Rationale: after `releaseDispatchClaim` deletes a row, a re-claim must reproduce the same id or a late webhook for a phantom-accepted message loses its correlation forever (Pitfall 4).
- **Secondary question — writer plurality on `dispatching -> reconciling`: `add-alongside`, accepted debt.** Two writers (send worker on ambiguous/interrupted; reconciler on stale-age sweep) are deliberate and recorded in the writer matrix. What would force a promote to a single arbitration function: a third writer appearing for that transition, or either writer needing to consult the other's state before writing.
- **Suggested (non-blocking) invariant test:** `send-state-machine.test.ts` asserts that `writersFor('dispatching', 'reconciling')` has exactly two entries and that every other transition has exactly one — encoding the generalized intent so a future third writer breaks a test rather than a production invariant.
