## Note on what boundaries 2 and 3 can and cannot distinguish

Boundaries 2 ("after SendGrid accepted") and 3 ("before the result was written") leave an **identical** ledger state: a committed `dispatching` claim with no terminal row. That is not a weakness of the tests — it is the fact the whole phase is built on. The platform genuinely cannot tell the two apart after the fact, which is precisely why the correct classification for both is `reconciling` rather than `failed` or `sent`.

The scenarios therefore differ in what happened *outside* the process, and assert different consequences: boundary 2's message was accepted, so a webhook eventually arrives and the reconciler resolves the row to `sent`; boundary 3's variants received a definite response (202 or 4xx) that was never recorded, and the 4xx variant demonstrates the accepted cost of at-most-once — a genuinely-rejected send whose result write was lost ends as `unknown`, not `failed`, because the platform will not assert an outcome it did not durably observe.
