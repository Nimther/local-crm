<objective>
Make the dead-letter path observable (WRK-10, D-08) by extending the established operator-watchdog pattern to a third consumer.

Purpose: a durable dead-letter record that nobody reads is not observability. This project already runs two watchdogs in the application process on the same shape — a periodic check, an atomic alert claim for deduplication, and a plain-text mail to the operator address through the platform key. D-08 makes the dead-letter table the third consumer of that twice-proven pattern; real alerting and a queue dashboard belong to the observability phase, and this is the interim signal that keeps the gap closed until then.
Output: the watchdog module, its boot wiring beside the existing two, and its tests.
</objective>

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| dead-letter record → operator mail | Alert content leaves the system; anything it carries is exposed to whoever holds the operator mailbox |
| two concurrent watchdog checks → one alert slot | Without an atomic claim, a restart or overlap could double-alert |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-12-10-01 | Information Disclosure | alert body | medium | mitigate | The alert carries only counts, queue names and a timestamp — never a payload field — so no job data leaves the system through this channel even though the stored payloads are already redacted. Asserted by the alert-body criterion. |
| T-12-10-02 | Denial of Service | alert flooding the operator mailbox | medium | mitigate | The atomic single-statement claim plus the dedup window bound alerts to one per window, and a failed send releases the claim rather than silently consuming it. |
| T-12-10-03 | Repudiation | terminal failures nobody sees | high | mitigate | The end-to-end case proves an exhausted job produces a durable row that the watchdog surfaces, so the observability claim rests on a test rather than on two independently-working halves. |
| T-12-10-04 | Elevation of Privilege | application process reading a platform table | low | accept | The watchdog reads the platform-scoped dead-letter tables through the application's ordinary connection, exactly as the two existing watchdogs read their health tables; no cross-tenant credential is involved and the scan role is granted nothing here. |
</threat_model>
