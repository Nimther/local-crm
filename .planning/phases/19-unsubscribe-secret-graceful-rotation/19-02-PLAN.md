<objective>
Wire `UNSUBSCRIBE_TOKEN_SECRET_PREVIOUS` through all three existing env-validation sites with full validation parity (D-02), tighten the charset contract on the primary secret too (D-03), and enforce the one structural bound code owns — a maximum previous-list length (D-07, SC4).

Purpose: `packages/delivery-core` splits the list on a comma and never validates it. Boot-time validation is what makes that split unambiguous and what turns SC4's "not an unbounded list" from a documented intention into an enforced fact. All three sites live in one plan on purpose: RESEARCH Pitfall 4 documents that this codebase's accepted triplication is only safe when the three are updated together.

Output: a zod schema, a worker boot assertion and a predev check that agree, each with executable coverage, plus a parity assertion that fails if any one of them drifts.
</objective>

## Resolved research questions

- **Open Question 1 (shared vs per-site max constant): hard-code independently at each site.** The codebase's own documented reasoning for triplication — each process validates its own env autonomously, there is no shared runtime config service (SPECIFICATION.md §3.1) — applies to this constant exactly as it already applies to the 32-character floor. Drift is handled by the parity assertion in Task 3, not by a cross-package import.
- **Open Question 2 (check-env.mjs treatment): conditional structural validation when the variable is set, no entry in `required`.** The variable is optional, so there is nothing to require a presence of. `check-env.mjs` is not purely presence-only — it already rejects an out-of-range `KMS_PROVIDER` *value* and exits 1. That is the precedent this follows, and it is what makes D-02's "enforced at all three sites" true rather than nominal.
