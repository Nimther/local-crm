<objective>
Prove SC3's first half the way this repo proves every other durability claim: with a real SIGKILL against a real child process, not a simulated failure. A purge is started against a workspace with enough data to need many batches, the process is killed mid-walk, and the next run resumes and completes — with destroyed-row counts that match an uninterrupted purge of identical data exactly, so the D-10 evidence record is truthful across the crash as well as after it.

Three seams are worth killing at, and they are different failures: mid-batch (does the DELETE-plus-checkpoint single commit really hold?), between tables (is `completed_tables` granular and correct?), and after the last table but before the tombstone and the auth step (does the purge finish the tail, or declare victory early?). The harness already exists — `erasure-scrub-resume.test.ts` does exactly this shape for the per-contact scrub, and sixteen sibling scenarios share the pattern.

This plan is deliberately last among the code plans. It is the one that would catch a checkpoint bug introduced by any of 22-01, 22-05 or 22-07, so it runs against the finished walk rather than a partial one. Its `files_modified` includes the worker and checkpoint modules defensively: if the proof exposes a real resumability bug, the fix belongs here rather than in a follow-up gap-closure plan.

Purpose: PRG-03 / SC3 — "a purge killed mid-run resumes and completes on the next run".
Output: the failure-injection scenario, its npm script, its place in `failure:all`, and whatever checkpoint fix the kill exposes.
</objective>
