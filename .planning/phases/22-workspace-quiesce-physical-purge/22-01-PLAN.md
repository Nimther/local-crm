<objective>
Prove the whole workspace-purge architecture end-to-end on the thinnest real path: a soft-deleted workspace whose retention has elapsed is discovered by a scheduled tick, announced (report-only tick, per-table census written to a new platform-level `purge_records` row), then on the NEXT tick destroyed through an ordered, batched, checkpointed two-table walk (`subscription_status_history` → `contacts`), and finally retired as an anonymized `organization` tombstone — with the `erasure_records` evidence for those very contacts still readable afterwards.

This is the phase's tracer. Two pieces are genuinely new and must be proven before anything is built on them: (1) the checkpoint has to live on a platform table because the tables it walks are being destroyed (`erasure-scrub-checkpoint.ts`'s storage location cannot be reused — RESEARCH.md "State of the Art"), and (2) the report-then-destroy state machine with its point of no return is what the restore CLI, the watchdog and every expansion plan hang off. Two tables — not one — because the per-table handoff in FK order *is* the architecture.

Purpose: PRG-01 (retention-triggered purge), PRG-02 (PII destroyed / evidence kept), PRG-03 (checkpointed outside tenant tables, replay-safe), PRG-05 (per-batch eligibility re-check that refuses) reach a working, tested state on one workspace before the remaining ~18 tables, the secrets, the auth rows and the quiesce layers are added.
Output: the `purge_records` schema + migration, the `erasure_records` FK-relax migration, the worker's first env-validation module, the purge worker with its checkpoint and batched-DELETE primitives, and an end-to-end integration test.
</objective>

Exit the loop when a page deletes zero rows, then confirm with `countPurgeTableRows`; if that is non-zero (rows were `SKIP LOCKED`), retry the loop up to 3 times and otherwise throw so BullMQ retries the job later.
