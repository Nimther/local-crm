<objective>
Close the discovery half of D-01 where it belongs — in Postgres. Three cross-workspace scan policies (`campaigns_scan`, `flows_scan`, `flow_runs_scan`) currently return rows for soft-deleted workspaces, so their consumers keep finding work to enqueue for a tenant that asked to be deleted. One migration re-creates all three with a soft-delete exclusion predicate; every current and future consumer of those policies inherits the fix without a line of application code.

Two research findings drive the shape and must not be trimmed. **`flow_runs_scan` is a third gap CONTEXT.md's own discovery pass did not name** — same policy shape, same missing predicate, consumed by `flow-reconciliation.worker.ts`. Left unpatched, a deleted workspace's flow runs keep waking and advancing for the entire 30-day retention window: the dispatch gate from plan 22-02 blocks the mail, but `current_node_id` and `exited_at` keep mutating, which directly contradicts D-02's promise that a restored workspace finds its flows exactly as the tenant left them. **And PT-03's premise is wrong**: migration 0042 already grants `mega_crm_scan` table-level `SELECT` on `organization`, so this is a pure predicate change with no grant migration at all.

Purpose: PRG-06 / SC1 — nothing new is ever enqueued for a soft-deleted workspace.
Output: migration 0070, the analytics-reconciliation enumeration filter (RESEARCH Open Question 3), and a negative-discovery test per policy with a live-workspace control beside it.
</objective>
