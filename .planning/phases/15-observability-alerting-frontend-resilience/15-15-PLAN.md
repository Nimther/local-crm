OPS-18 / D-12: analytics that tell the truth about their own freshness, in both the normal and the degraded case.

Output: an always-visible data-as-of label, a conditional delay banner, and both wired into every analytics view.
</objective>

  <behavior>
    - Given a watermark and no lag, the label renders the timestamp and the banner does not render.
    - Given a lag below the threshold, the banner does not render and the label still renders.
    - Given a lag above the threshold, the banner renders with the delayed message and the label still renders.
    - Given a null watermark, the label renders a no-data-yet message and the banner does not render.
    - Exactly at the threshold does not render the banner; strictly above does.
    - The label's timestamp is formatted in the viewer's local time, not in UTC, with the same formatting helper the rest of the app uses.
  </behavior>

    Create `StaleDataBanner.tsx`: an amber, non-dismissible banner rendered only when the API-reported lag exceeds a single exported threshold constant with a rationale comment. Its copy states that analytics is delayed and that recent activity may not be included. It sits above the numbers and never replaces them — labelled numbers are more useful than hidden ones.
    Both are presentational: they take values and render. They do not fetch, do not read the router, and do not compute staleness beyond comparing the API-supplied lag against the one threshold. Staleness is never inferred from how old the data is — that would flag every quiet tenant.
